# Systems Atlas Project

## Brand identity system — v1.0

**Primary application:** systemsatlasproject.com  
**Secondary application:** Systems Atlas Project accounts on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube  
**Status:** primary symbol adopted. Typography and website implementation remain provisional.

---

## 1. Brand hierarchy

Systems Atlas Project is the principal intellectual project.

The atlas decomposes biological, academic, economic, healthcare, legal, meta, political, religious and technological systems into their smallest defensible parts. The long-term aim is not only a structural map, but a functional model: change one node and observe how the consequences propagate.

Konki, Shutoku, Bottou and Kantetsu are independent tools precipitated from particular parts of the map. They fund and evidence the method. They are not the definition of Systems Atlas, and their existing identities should not be collapsed into the parent identity.

### Naming

- **Formal name:** Systems Atlas Project
- **Ordinary name:** Systems Atlas
- **Domain:** systemsatlasproject.com
- Never abbreviate the public name to SAP.

### Recommended primary line

> A map of how systems work.

This describes the destination without pretending the map is already complete.

### Supporting explanation

> Systems Atlas decomposes the systems that shape the world into their smallest defensible parts. The next step is a model: change one part and watch the consequences propagate.

---

## 2. Brand character

The identity should feel:

- Rigorous, without sounding academic
- Ambitious, without making grandiose claims
- Cartographic, without becoming nostalgic
- Technical, without imitating a software company
- Built by one person, without presenting itself as a personal blog
- Incomplete by design: a live map under construction

The useful reference is a contemporary public cartographic project or measuring instrument—not a productivity brand, wellness product, startup or adventure map.

## 3. Primary symbol: Repaired Paths

The adopted mark is a dark rounded field containing three nested, interrupted pathways. The paths use softened rectangular geometry rather than perfect circles. Open breaks remain visible, while coloured sections reconstruct other parts of the mapped structure.

It is not a literal topographic map. It is a compact model of the project’s method: systems within systems, incomplete knowledge, distinct forms of intervention, and explicit work at the boundaries.

### Meaning

- **The rounded field** is the current object of inquiry. A map always has a scope.
- **The nested pathways** are recursive levels of decomposition: system, component and subcomponent.
- **The cream sections** are structure that has been mapped strongly enough to state.
- **The open breaks** are uncertainty, disputed boundaries or work not yet completed.
- **The coloured sections** are different forms of work applied directly to the structure. Each occupies the path rather than sitting above or beside it.
- **The incomplete whole** prevents the project from claiming false completeness.
- **The dark field** represents the unmapped space around what is currently known. Practically, it also gives the mark unusually strong recognition at small sizes.

The mark is internally called **Repaired Paths**. That name does not need to appear in public copy.

### Colour within the mark

The wider interface palette describes four kinds of work:

- **Evidence Blue:** observation, measurement and external evidence
- **Structural Orange:** definition, classification and decomposition
- **Applied Green:** implementation, tools and tested intervention
- **Revision Magenta:** uncertainty, correction and active reconsideration

These meanings remain stable wherever the colours are used. The permanent logo uses all four because the atlas depends on all four forms of work. Their distribution is deliberately irregular: the symbol is not a key or progress chart, and colour does not divide the paths into equal stages.

### Construction rules

- Use three nested pathways with softened rectangular geometry.
- Keep the pathways visibly distinct; do not regularise them into perfect concentric circles.
- Use one consistent visual line weight across the mark.
- Preserve the established open breaks, including the left-facing opening of the innermost path.
- Preserve the two enlarged upper breaks: one in the upper-left outer path and one slightly right of centre in the upper middle path.
- Repairs must occupy the exact path of the structural line and match its width.
- Repairs must always be shorter than neighbouring cream sections.
- Never place two repair colours directly against one another; separate them with cream structure.
- Preserve the established balance of Evidence Blue, Structural Orange, Applied Green and Revision Magenta.
- Do not add a central point. The project has no privileged centre.
- Do not redraw the pathways as circles, a target, radar, Wi-Fi, a loading symbol or Activity-style rings.
- Do not align every break into one decorative slash; the interruptions represent different boundary problems at different levels.
- Maintain generous internal clear space. Do not add text inside the icon.
- Preserve sufficient inset for circular platform crops; no structural or coloured section may be clipped.
- At 64 px and below, use the full mark unless testing demonstrates that a simplified micro mark is necessary.

### Exclusions

Do not use:

- A treasure-map X
- Globes, atlases, open books or map pins
- Compass roses
- Brains, circuit boards or generic network diagrams
- Infinity symbols
- Mountains or scenic topography
- Japanese visual motifs
- Dense pseudo-scientific detail
- Decorative contour lines that imply terrain which does not exist
- Routes, waypoints or survey marks without a defined cartographic function

### Social profile icon

The icon must:

- Remain identifiable at 48 px
- Survive a circular crop with at least 12% clear space on every side
- Use the Repaired Paths master on Night Field
- Use no text
- Preserve all four repair colours and the established open breaks
- Keep every repair precisely aligned with the cream structure
- Avoid grids, shadows and secondary detail
- Use the final 1024 px PNG master for platform uploads

### Logo variants

- **Primary:** Mapped Structure with all four signal colours on Night Field
- **Micro:** derive only if testing proves the full mark fails below 64 px
- **Reversed one-colour:** Mapped Structure on Night Field
- **Dark one-colour:** Night Field structure on Chart Paper
- **Wordmark lockup:** symbol to the left of “Systems Atlas Project”; never place the name inside the symbol

The primary version is the default for social profiles and website mastheads. One-colour versions are for constrained production, not a stylistic alternative.

## 4. Colour

The palette begins with the selected dark identity rather than with paper. It remains functional: each vivid colour names a kind of work, not an app or a decorative mood. All four signal colours appear in the permanent mark as structural repairs.

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Night Field | `#101820` | Primary logo field, dark website sections and diagram backgrounds |
| Mapped Structure | `#F5F0E6` | Established structure on Night Field |
| Chart Paper | `#F1EEE5` | Long-form pages and light website sections |
| Evidence Blue | `#20A4FF` | Measurement, observation and external evidence |
| Structural Orange | `#FF6A00` | Definition, classification and decomposition |
| Applied Green | `#19D18B` | Implemented tools and tested intervention |
| Revision Magenta | `#FF2F87` | Correction, uncertainty and active reconsideration |

### Rules

- Night Field is the default dark surface and primary text colour on Chart Paper.
- Mapped Structure is the default text and line colour on Night Field.
- Vivid colours are graphical signals. Do not use them for body copy.
- Use one vivid colour for most interface states; use several only where the categories are genuinely present. The primary logo is the deliberate exception.
- Never recolour the four apps to match this palette. Their identities remain independent.
- Avoid gradients, glow, translucency and soft pastel versions of the signal colours.

### Relationship and certainty

Use line form before adding more colours:

- **Solid cream:** mapped structure
- **Open interruption:** unresolved or unexamined boundary
- **Coloured replacement:** active work reconnecting that boundary
- **Dashed relation:** inferred or conditional connection between nodes
- **Dotted relation:** proposed or not yet tested

Every diagram must include a key when more than one line form appears.

## 5. Typography — provisional

### Display and navigation

**IBM Plex Sans Condensed**

Use for the wordmark, large headings, navigation and compact map labels. Its condensed construction recalls space-constrained cartographic labelling without imitating historical maps.

### Body

**Atkinson Hyperlegible Next**

Use for body copy, explanations and controls. Prioritise character distinction and comfortable reading over fashion.

### Data and diagnostics

**IBM Plex Mono**

Use for node identifiers, coordinates, versions, dates, measurements and diagnostic metadata. Do not use mono for long prose.

### Hierarchy

- Display headings: condensed, sentence case
- Page title: 56–80 px desktop; 40–52 px mobile
- Section title: 32–44 px desktop; 28–36 px mobile
- Body: 18–20 px desktop; 17–19 px mobile
- Utility and map labels: 12–14 px
- Uppercase is reserved for short coordinates, classifications and navigation labels
- Avoid long passages in italics. Italic may encode one deliberate class of map information.

## 6. Layout

### Base interval

Use an 8 px mapping interval:

`4 / 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 96`

If a visible grid is used, it follows a 32 px or 64 px interval and must carry an interface function. The logo itself never sits on a decorative grid.

### Page behaviour

- Maximum reading width: 720 px
- Maximum full layout width: 1440 px
- Prefer rules, spacing and field changes to floating cards
- Content corners should be square or subtly rounded, generally 0–8 px
- Reserve the strongly rounded square for the logo and explicitly bounded map views
- Avoid shadows, glass effects and pill-shaped controls
- Use asymmetry deliberately, but keep text alignment stable

### Light and dark rhythm

- Use Night Field for the hero, map visualisations, diagnostic states and major transitions.
- Use Chart Paper for explanation, method, sources and longer reading.
- Do not make the entire website dark. The alternation expresses the movement between unknown space and stated structure.
- A vivid signal colour should normally occupy less than 10% of a page.

## 7. Website structure

The home page must establish the atlas before introducing the apps.

### 1. Header

- Systems Atlas Project wordmark
- Map
- Method
- Tools
- Diagnostics
- About

### 2. Hero

**Headline:** A map of how systems work.

Use a live or gently animated structural fragment rather than a decorative hero image. The graphic should show at least one boundary, relation and selected node.

The first screen should communicate:

- This is a map of systems
- It is being built
- It is intended to become a model

### 3. Destination

Explain the progression:

**Structure → function → propagation**

Lead with the destination. Evidence it with the method.

### 4. Current map

Show the nine current L0 domains and the state of their decomposition. Progress means mapped structure and documented uncertainty—not a gamified completion percentage.

### 5. Method

Explain:

- Mutually exclusive divisions
- Collective exhaustiveness within cognitive limits
- Equal abstraction
- Definitions, inclusion and exclusion criteria
- Sources and boundary cases
- The fixed analytical lenses
- Diagnostics and recorded uncertainty

### 6. Tools from the map

Present Konki, Shutoku, Bottou and Kantetsu only after the atlas and method.

- Retain each app's native logo and colour
- Place each inside a neutral Systems Atlas frame
- Describe the system node or problem from which it precipitated
- Do not imply shared data or forced suite membership
- Website-side attribution may read: **A tool from Systems Atlas Project**

### 7. Evidence and diagnostics

Expose sources, unresolved boundaries, corrections and methodological changes. This is a core credibility surface, not a legal appendix.

### 8. Footer

- Built by one person
- No advertising
- No outside money
- No attention farming

## 8. Interface components

### Nodes

Every public node representation should support:

- Name
- Level and parent
- Definition
- Inclusion criteria
- Exclusion criteria
- Sources
- Boundary cases
- Current diagnostic state
- Available lenses

### Diagram panels

- Flat fields, not cards with elevation
- Visible scale or abstraction level
- A legend whenever encoding is not self-evident
- Source access adjacent to the claim it supports
- Selected nodes use Revision Magenta
- Hover and focus cannot rely on colour alone

### Buttons and links

- Primary action: Night Field fill on Chart Paper
- Secondary action: transparent with a 1–2 px Night Field rule
- Links: underlined in prose
- Avoid vague labels such as “Discover” or “Explore”
- Prefer concrete labels: “Open the map”, “Read the method”, “View sources”

---

## 9. Voice

The register is plain, declarative and slightly dry.

### Use

- Short sentences
- Concrete nouns
- Active voice
- Explicit limits
- Named trade-offs
- Understatement

### Avoid

- Revolutionary, reimagining, unleash, supercharge, game-changing
- Ecosystem, holistic, seamless, journey
- Manufactured urgency
- Exclamation marks
- “We” when the project means one person
- Claims that the atlas is complete

### Test

If a sentence would sound wrong printed in a technical manual, it is off-brand.

## 10. Social identity

### Profile

- **Display name:** Systems Atlas Project
- **Avatar:** Repaired Paths primary mark
- **Bio:** Mapping how systems work. Building the smallest useful tools from the map.
- **Link:** systemsatlasproject.com

### Content formats

1. **Map note** — one node, boundary or relationship
2. **Method note** — one defensible classification decision
3. **Diagnostic** — an uncertainty, correction or failed assumption
4. **Tool precipitation** — how a concrete app emerged from part of the map
5. **Propagation study** — what may change downstream when one node changes

Every post should identify its abstraction level and distinguish evidence from inference.
