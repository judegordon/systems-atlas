§ 01 How it schedules

The interval is the whole product.

A review that comes too early wastes the repetition. One that comes too late is not a review, it is relearning. Shutoku tracks how securely you know each word and places the next sighting at the point where recall is about to fail.

Words you hold well recede. Words you keep losing come back sooner. You do not set any of this, and there is nothing to tune.

Spaced repetition

The scheduler works from your own answer history for each individual word, not from a fixed ladder of intervals applied to everything.

Verb conjugation

A full conjugation engine covering て-form, た-form, negative, potential, causative and passive. Any verb in the dictionary can be drilled in any of its forms rather than memorised as a fixed dictionary entry.

Dictionary

Vocabulary across JLPT N5 to N1, searchable by reading, by meaning, or by level. You can filter reviews down to the band you are actually sitting.

Kana and romaji

Hiragana, katakana and romaji, switchable at any point. Romaji is a ramp, not a destination, and the app assumes you will want to leave it behind.

Progress

Daily review counts, retention rate, and vocabulary growth over time. Enough to tell whether the schedule is working, and not so much that it becomes the thing you look at instead of studying.

Sync

An account carries your vocabulary, progress and review schedule between iPhone, iPad and Mac. The app is fully usable without one.

§ 02 On the name

習得

Shutoku — to acquire through study and practice. To make something yours rather than to hold it temporarily. That is the distinction between knowing a word and owning it, and the scheduler exists to move words across that line.

The Japanese naming is not decoration. English has no single unclaimed word for this.
